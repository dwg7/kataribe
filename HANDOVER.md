## Primary-source material for the first dossier: 十勝岳大正泥流 (1926-05-24)

Gathered 2026-09-05 from GSI's official 1:50,000 火山土地条件図「十勝岳」 explanatory document (published 平成2年/1990, surveyed 平成元年/1989 — PDF: https://www.gsi.go.jp/common/000109832.pdf). **Copyright note**: the document's own printed notice says 複製を禁ずる, but hfu confirmed Japan's government standard terms of use partially supersede this, and he holds his own existing usage approval (as with `kitavolca`'s 測量法 approval) — treat this as citation/adaptation of the underlying facts, not reproduction of GSI's document itself. Cite the source in whatever dossier file actually uses these facts.

**The core narrative** (hazard layer):
- 十勝岳 (Tokachidake), 2,077m, part of the 大雪–十勝 volcanic chain (Hokkaido's central-north, ~80km NE–SW ridge of 1,500–2,000m peaks). Active volcano; major activity concentrated on the northwest slope just below the summit.
- 1926-05-24: a major eruption's explosion destroyed the northwest half of the central crater dome (中央火口丘). The eruption's heat melted residual snow (残雪) — this was a **snowmelt-triggered secondary mudflow (二次泥流)**, not a direct pyroclastic flow.
- The mudflow split into two channels, down the 美瑛川 (Biei River) and 富良野川 (Furano River).
- It reached the lowland area around **上富良野町 (Kamifurano)** in just **25–26 minutes** after the explosion — almost no warning time.
- Deposits piled 1m+ thick near 望岳台 (Bōgakudai), south side, ~1,100m elevation (cited source within the document: 石川ほか, 1971).
- **Death toll: 144 dead/missing** on 1926-05-24, plus 2 missing from a separate explosion on 1926-05-08. Minor eruptions continued afterward.
- Precursor timeline that year (per the document's eruption chronology table): Feb 1926 — sand/gravel ejection; Apr 1926 — ashfall, earthquake swarms; May 13–14 — an initial, smaller mudflow; May 24 — the major eruption and mudflow described above. (So there *was* a roughly 3-month build-up with visible precursors, unlike a no-warning scenario — worth getting this detail right in the dossier rather than assuming a sudden, unheralded event.)
- The 1926 crater is still named **大正火口** (Taisho Crater) today — the event is literally written into the landform's name.
- The 1926 deposits were still visually identifiable in 1988 aerial photography (63 years later), labeled and distinguished from a separate, smaller 1988 mudflow (~600m flow) in the same document — a useful lower-magnitude comparison case if a second dossier or a "how does this compare" angle is wanted later.
- **The map's own design already treats this event as the reference case for future risk**: it overlays the 1926 mudflow's reach area together with 10-year maximum snow-depth data, specifically to support estimating future snowmelt-triggered mudflow risk. This is a strong, authoritative anchor for the hazard layer's framing — GSI itself designed this map around exactly this event as the worked example.

**The livelihood/place-identity layer** (why 十勝岳大正泥流 is a good first case for testing layer-selection, per hfu and the design proposal): the same deposit area that the 1926 lahar covered is, today, farmland in the Kamifurano/Furano area — disaster and livelihood are literally the same physical land, at different points in its history. This is the concrete instance of "layered significance on the same coordinates" the whole `kataribe` concept is built around — see `CLAUDE.md`.

**Not yet gathered**: specific present-day agricultural/tourism facts about the Kamifurano area (crop types, any commemorative sites, whether there's an existing local memorial or annual observance — 2026 being the 100th anniversary makes this worth checking), and confirmation of exactly which kitavolca/stars.optgeo.org map link(s) best represent this specific event's extent (kitavolca's `vlcm.pmtiles` covers 十勝岳 generally — a real link needs the right bbox/style parameters, not just "some Tokachi map").

## Design clarification from reading ADR 0010 directly (2026-09-08)

[ADR 0010](https://github.com/UNopenGIS/staccato-spec/blob/main/spec/adr/0010-narrative-sequencing.md) is live (verified by reading the actual text, not just `staccato-ecosystem-85`'s relay — no discrepancies found; it cites this repo's `DECISIONS.md` D2/D3/D4 accurately). Its §3 says a bare Map Intent (no wrapper) is only appropriate when there's *no caption to carry*. Taken literally, the first dossier's hazard layer *does* have caption-worthy content (`map_caveat`), which would suggest it needs a captioned single-step wrapper, not the bare `#q=` link this repo actually built for it (`dossiers/tokachidake-taisho-mudflow-1926.json`'s `cartographer_links.spiccato`).

**Resolution**: this isn't actually a conflict, once kataribe's real UX shape is considered. `spiccato` still has no caption-rendering feature at all (confirmed by the spiccato session, 2026-09-05/07). But kataribe's Staff never needs the Cartographer page itself to display the caption — the caption/caveat is delivered **in Staff's own conversational reply**, alongside the map link, not embedded in the URL or rendered on the Cartographer page. A dossier layer's `map_caveat` exists to make sure Staff *says* the right caution when handing over the link, not to be baked into the link itself. This is a legitimate design choice within ADR 0009's "narrative-style formats remain outside this spec's compliance floor" — kataribe is choosing not to build a captioned-wrapper Cartographer artifact at all, for any layer, relying on the chat channel instead. Worth keeping in mind when the Staff prompt gets written (next major task): it must actually carry each layer's `map_caveat` into its own response text, not just hand over the bare link.
